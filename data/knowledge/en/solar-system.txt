ENCYCLOPEDIA OF THE SOLAR SYSTEM

Document Version: 1.0
Format: Text Reference of Astronomical Data
Language: English

The Solar System is a gravitationally bound planetary system comprising the central star 
(the Sun) and all natural celestial bodies orbiting it: 8 major planets, 5 officially 
recognized dwarf planets (and dozens of candidates), hundreds of moons, hundreds of 
thousands of asteroids, comets, centaurs, meteoroids, and vast reservoirs of icy bodies 
(the Kuiper Belt, Scattered Disc, and Oort Cloud).

Age of the Solar System: ~4.5682 billion years (determined via uranium-lead dating 
of the oldest calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions [CAIs] in meteorites).
Location: Orion Arm of the Milky Way Galaxy, ~26,000 light-years (8 kpc) from the Galactic Center.
Galactic orbital period (cosmic year): 225-250 million years.
Orbital speed around the Galactic Center: ~220-240 km/s.


1. CENTRAL BODY: THE SUN (SOL)

Spectral Type: G2V (main-sequence yellow dwarf)
Mass fraction of the Solar System: 99.866%

Key Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 1.9885 × 10^30 kg (~332,946 Earth masses)
- Mean radius: 696,342 km (~109 Earth radii)
- Mean density: 1.408 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: 274.0 m/s² (~27.9 g)
- Escape velocity (at surface): 617.7 km/s
- Luminosity: 3.828 × 10^26 W
- Photospheric temperature: ~5,778 K (5,505 °C)
- Core temperature: ~15.7 million K (~1.57 × 10^7 °C)
- Corona temperature: 1-3 million K
- Differential rotation period:
  * at equator: 25.05 Earth days
  * at poles: 34.4 Earth days

Chemical Composition (by mass in the photosphere):
- Hydrogen (H): ~73.46%
- Helium (He): ~24.85%
- Oxygen (O): ~0.77%
- Carbon (C): ~0.29%
- Iron (Fe): ~0.16%
- Neon (Ne): ~0.12%
- Nitrogen (N): ~0.09%
- Silicon (Si): ~0.07%
- Magnesium (Mg): ~0.05%
- Sulfur (S): ~0.04%

Internal Structure:
1. Thermonuclear Core (0 - 0.25 R_sol): density up to 150 g/cm³, site of the proton-proton 
   chain reaction (fusing 1H into 4He). Every second, ~600 million tons of hydrogen are converted 
   into 596 million tons of helium, with 4.26 million tons of mass converted directly into energy via E = mc².
2. Radiative Zone (0.25 - 0.7 R_sol): energy is transported outward by high-energy photons through 
   repeated absorption and re-emission. A photon takes between 10,000 and 170,000 years to reach the convective zone.
3. Tachocline: a thin transition shear layer with strong rotational velocity gradient; believed 
   to be the seat of the solar magnetic dynamo.
4. Convective Zone (0.7 - 1.0 R_sol): energy transport via turbulent plasma convection cells 
   (Bénard cells). Manifests at the surface as granulation.
5. Atmosphere:
   - Photosphere: visible "surface" ~300-500 km thick, responsible for the absorption line spectrum.
   - Chromosphere: layer ~2,000 km thick with temperatures ranging from 4,500 K to 20,000 K.
   - Transition Region: abrupt temperature jump from tens of thousands to millions of kelvins.
   - Corona: extended outer atmosphere and source of the solar wind.

Solar Cycles:
- Schwabe Cycle (sunspot number): ~11 years.
- Hale Cycle (full magnetic polarity reversal): ~22 years.



2. INNER REGION: TERRESTRIAL PLANETS

The terrestrial (rocky) planets are characterized by high density, silicate-metallic composition, 
solid surfaces, and few or no natural satellites.


2.1. MERCURY

The closest planet to the Sun and the smallest major planet in the Solar System.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 57.91 million km (0.3871 AU)
- Perihelion: 46.00 million km (0.3075 AU)
- Aphelion: 69.82 million km (0.4667 AU)
- Orbital eccentricity: 0.2056 (highest among major planets)
- Orbital inclination to ecliptic: 7.00°
- Sidereal orbital period: 87.969 Earth days
- Sidereal rotation period: 58.646 Earth days
- Orbital resonance: 3:2 spin-orbit resonance (3 rotations on its axis for every 2 orbits)
- Solar day: 175.94 Earth days

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 3.3011 × 10^23 kg (0.055 Earth masses)
- Equatorial radius: 2,439.7 km (0.383 Earth radii)
- Mean density: 5.427 g/cm³ (second densest major planet after Earth)
- Surface gravity: 3.7 m/s² (0.38 g)
- Escape velocity: 4.25 km/s
- Albedo: 0.142
- Surface temperature: from -180 °C (night) to +430 °C (day at subsolar point)

Internal Structure:
- Huge iron-nickel core with radius ~2,000 km (~85% of planet's radius), partially liquid 
  (generates a weak dipole magnetic field ~1.1% of Earth's).
- Thin silicate mantle and crust 400-500 km thick.

Atmosphere (Exosphere):
Extremely tenuous exosphere with surface pressure less than 10^-14 bar.
Composition: oxygen (42%), sodium (29%), hydrogen (22%), helium (6%), potassium (0.5%).

Terrain and Geology:
- Heavily cratered surface with impact basins (Caloris Basin / Caloris Planitia, diameter ~1,550 km).
- Lobate scarps (cliffs up to 3 km high and hundreds of kilometers long) formed as the planet cooled 
  and shrank (reducing radius by 5-10 km).
- Water ice confirmed by radar observations in permanently shadowed polar craters.


2.2. VENUS

Second planet from the Sun, often called "Earth's twin" in size and mass, but dominated by a runaway greenhouse effect.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 108.21 million km (0.7233 AU)
- Eccentricity: 0.0067 (most circular orbit of any planet)
- Inclination to ecliptic: 3.39°
- Sidereal orbital period: 224.701 Earth days
- Sidereal rotation period: 243.025 Earth days (retrograde rotation!)
- Solar day: 116.75 Earth days
- Axial tilt: 177.36° (clockwise rotation when viewed from celestial north)

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 4.8675 × 10^24 kg (0.815 Earth masses)
- Equatorial radius: 6,051.8 km (0.949 Earth radii)
- Mean density: 5.243 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: 8.87 m/s² (0.904 g)
- Escape velocity: 10.36 km/s
- Bond albedo: 0.76 (cloud deck reflects most incoming sunlight)
- Surface temperature: nearly uniform across the planet at ~464 °C (737 K), hotter than Mercury's surface.
- Surface atmospheric pressure: 92-93 bar (~9.2-9.3 MPa, equivalent to depth of 900 m in Earth's oceans).

Atmosphere:
- Carbon dioxide (CO2): 96.5%
- Nitrogen (N2): 3.5%
- Sulfur dioxide (SO2): 150 ppm
- Argon (Ar): 70 ppm
- Water vapor (H2O): 20 ppm
- Dense cloud deck located at altitudes of 48-65 km composed of droplets of 75-85% sulfuric acid (H2SO4).
- Atmospheric super-rotation: high-altitude winds circle the planet in just 4 Earth days (speeds up to 100 m/s).

Geology and Surface Features:
- Basaltic volcanic plains, volcanic domes, shield volcanoes (Maat Mons, height 8 km), tesserae (highly deformed highlands).
- No plate tectonics in the terrestrial sense; episodic global resurfacing driven by catastrophic volcanic episodes is suspected.
- Intrinsic dipole magnetic field is virtually absent due to slow rotation or lack of internal core convection; an induced magnetosphere is created by interaction with the solar wind.


2.3. EARTH (EARTH / TERRA) AND THE MOON

The only known world in the Universe confirmed to harbor life.

Earth Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 149.598 million km (1.00000011 AU)
- Perihelion: 147.098 million km (0.9833 AU)
- Aphelion: 152.098 million km (1.0167 AU)
- Orbital eccentricity: 0.0167
- Obliquity to orbit (axial tilt): 23.44° (drives the cycle of seasons)
- Sidereal orbital period: 365.256 Earth days
- Sidereal day (rotation period): 23 hours 56 minutes 4.091 seconds

Earth Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 5.9722 × 10^24 kg
- Equatorial radius: 6,378.137 km
- Polar radius: 6,356.752 km (flattening: 1/298.257)
- Mean density: 5.514 g/cm³ (densest planet in the Solar System)
- Surface gravity: 9.80665 m/s²
- Escape velocity: 11.186 km/s
- Magnetic field: dipole field, surface intensity 30-60 µT, generated by geodynamo in liquid outer core.

Earth Internal Structure:
- Crust: continental (30-70 km, granites/sediments) and oceanic (5-10 km, basalts).
- Mantle: upper and lower (down to 2,900 km, peridotite, bridgmanite, ferropericlase).
- Outer Core: liquid Fe-Ni with light elements (Si, S, O), depth 2,900-5,150 km.
- Inner Core: solid crystalline Fe-Ni sphere of radius ~1,220 km, temperature ~5,700 K.
- Hydrosphere: covers ~70.8% of planetary surface (total water volume ~1.386 billion km³).
- Atmosphere (at surface by volume of dry air):
  * Nitrogen (N2): 78.084%
  * Oxygen (O2): 20.946%
  * Argon (Ar): 0.934%
  * Carbon dioxide (CO2): ~0.042% (420+ ppm)
  * Neon, helium, methane, krypton, hydrogen, and variable water vapor (0-4%).

Natural Satellite - The Moon:
- Semi-major axis: 384,400 km (0.00257 AU)
- Eccentricity: 0.0549
- Sidereal month: 27.3217 days (synchronous rotation: tidally locked with one face to Earth)
- Synodic month (lunar phases): 29.5306 days
- Mass: 7.342 × 10^22 kg (0.0123 Earth masses, 1/81.3)
- Mean radius: 1,737.4 km (0.273 Earth radii)
- Mean density: 3.344 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: 1.62 m/s² (0.165 g)
- Escape velocity: 2.38 km/s
- Surface: lunar maria (dark basaltic lava plains with low albedo) and lunar highlands 
  (anorthositic ancient cratered terrain).
- Origin: giant impact hypothesis (collision of proto-Earth with Mars-sized protoplanet Theia ~4.5 billion years ago).


2.4. MARS AND ITS MOONS

The fourth planet from the Sun, the outermost terrestrial planet. Known as the "Red Planet" 
due to ubiquitous iron(III) oxide (Fe2O3, rust) in its regolith.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 227.94 million km (1.5237 AU)
- Perihelion: 206.65 million km (1.3814 AU)
- Aphelion: 249.23 million km (1.6660 AU)
- Eccentricity: 0.0934 (causes significant seasonal climate asymmetry)
- Axial tilt: 25.19° (seasonal variation similar to Earth's)
- Sidereal orbital period: 686.98 Earth days (1.88 Earth years)
- Solar day (sol): 24 hours 39 minutes 35.244 seconds

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 6.4171 × 10^23 kg (0.107 Earth masses)
- Equatorial radius: 3,396.2 km (0.532 Earth radii)
- Polar radius: 3,376.2 km
- Mean density: 3.933 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: 3.72 m/s² (0.379 g)
- Escape velocity: 5.03 km/s
- Mean surface temperature: about -63 °C (extremes from -140 °C to +20 °C)
- Surface atmospheric pressure: 6.1 mbar (0.006 atm)

Atmospheric Composition:
- Carbon dioxide (CO2): 95.32%
- Nitrogen (N2): 1.9%
- Argon (Ar): 1.6%
- Oxygen (O2): 0.13%
- Carbon monoxide (CO): 0.08%
- Water vapor: 0.02%
- Frequent planet-encircling global dust storms.

Prominent Geological Structures:
- Olympus Mons: highest shield volcano in the Solar System. 
  Height: 21.9 km above datum, base diameter: ~600 km.
- Valles Marineris: colossal rift canyon system. 
  Length: >4,000 km, width: up to 200 km, depth: up to 7-11 km.
- Crustal Dichotomy: smooth northern lowlands (possible ancient ocean basin) 
  versus rugged, heavily cratered southern highlands.
- Polar Caps: permanent water ice covered by seasonal layers of frozen carbon dioxide ("dry ice").

Moons of Mars:
1. Phobos ("fear"):
   - Dimensions: 26.8 × 22.4 × 18.4 km (triaxial ellipsoid)
   - Semi-major axis: 9,376 km (only ~6,000 km from Martian surface)
   - Orbital period: 7 h 39 min (faster than Mars' rotation: rises in the west, sets in the east)
   - Orbit decaying by ~1.8 m per century; expected to break up into a ring or impact Mars in 30-50 million years.
   - Prominent feature: Stickney Crater (~9 km across).
2. Deimos ("terror"):
   - Dimensions: 15.0 × 12.2 × 10.4 km
   - Semi-major axis: 23,463 km
   - Orbital period: 30 h 18 min
   - Thick blanket of regolith that subdues impact topography.



3. MAIN ASTEROID BELT

Toroidal region located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter (spanning 2.1 to 3.3 AU).
Total belt mass is modest: ~2.39 × 10^21 kg (~4% of the Moon's mass).
Over half of this mass is concentrated in four large bodies: Ceres, Vesta, Pallas, and Hygiea.
The belt features Kirkwood gaps-depleted orbital zones cleared by orbital resonances with Jupiter.

Asteroid Spectral Types:
- C-type (carbonaceous): ~75% of asteroids, very dark (albedo 0.03-0.09), rich in carbon compounds and hydrated minerals.
- S-type (silicate/stony): ~17%, albedo 0.10-0.22, composed of magnesium and iron silicates.
- M-type (metallic): composed primarily of metallic iron and nickel.


3.1. DWARF PLANET CERES (1 CERES)

Discovered on January 1, 1801, by Giuseppe Piazzi. Classified as a dwarf planet in 2006.
Explored by NASA's Dawn spacecraft (2015-2018).

Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 413.7 million km (2.767 AU)
- Orbital period: 4.60 years (1,681 days)
- Equatorial diameter: 965 km, polar: 891 km (oblate spheroid in hydrostatic equilibrium)
- Mass: 9.39 × 10^20 kg (~31% of total Main Belt mass)
- Mean density: 2.16 g/cm³
- Rotation period: 9.07 hours
- Internal structure: differentiated into a rocky core and water-ice-rich mantle/crust.
- Features: cryovolcanism (Ahuna Mons, height 4 km), bright sodium carbonate salt deposits 
  in Occator Crater (Cerealia and Vinalia Faculae).


3.2. LARGEST ASTEROIDS OF THE BELT

4 Vesta (4 Vesta):
- Dimensions: 578 × 560 × 458 km
- Mass: 2.59 × 10^20 kg (~9% of belt mass)
- Differentiated rocky body with metallic core, basaltic crust, and massive Rheasilvia impact crater 
  (diameter 505 km, central peak 22 km high). Source of HED basaltic meteorites.

2 Pallas (2 Pallas):
- Dimensions: 568 × 530 × 477 km
- Mass: 2.05 × 10^20 kg (~7% of belt mass)
- Highly inclined orbit to ecliptic: 34.8°

10 Hygiea (10 Hygiea):
- Diameter: 434 km (nearly spherical shape; candidate dwarf planet)
- Mass: 8.3 × 10^19 kg (~3% of belt mass)
- Carbonaceous (C-type), extremely low albedo (~0.07).



4. OUTER REGION: GIANT PLANETS

The gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn) consist predominantly of hydrogen and helium.
The ice giants (Uranus, Neptune) consist predominantly of heavier volatiles / "ices" 
(water, ammonia, methane in high-temperature, high-pressure supercritical fluids) beneath hydrogen-helium envelopes.


4.1. JUPITER

The largest planet in the Solar System, hosting a miniature "solar system" of 95 known moons (as of 2024-2026).

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 778.57 million km (5.2044 AU)
- Orbital period: 11.862 Earth years (4,332.6 days)
- Eccentricity: 0.0489
- Axial tilt: only 3.13° (negligible seasonal change)
- Rotation period: 9 hours 55 minutes 30 seconds (fastest rotator among all planets!)

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 1.8982 × 10^27 kg (317.8 Earth masses, 2.48 times the mass of all other planets combined)
- Equatorial radius: 71,492 km (11.21 Earth radii)
- Polar radius: 66,854 km (pronounced oblateness: 0.0648)
- Mean density: 1.326 g/cm³
- Surface gravity (at 1 bar level): 24.79 m/s² (2.53 g)
- Escape velocity: 59.5 km/s
- Magnetic field: strongest planetary magnetic field; dipole moment is 18,000 times that of Earth. 
  The sunward magnetosphere extends 5 million km, and its magnetotail extends past the orbit of Saturn.

Atmospheric Composition:
- Hydrogen (H2): ~89.8%
- Helium (He): ~10.2%
- Methane (CH4): 0.3%
- Ammonia (NH3): 0.026%
- Trace gases: ethane, water, phosphine, hydrogen sulfide.
- Cloud bands: alternating dark belts and light zones with complex circulation dynamics.
- Great Red Spot (GRS): persistent anticyclonic storm observed for over 350 years. 
  Dimensions: ~16,000 × 12,000 km (larger than Earth), peripheral wind speeds up to 430-680 km/h.

Internal Structure:
1. Outer molecular hydrogen-helium envelope.
2. Layer of liquid metallic hydrogen (forming at pressures >1-2 million atm and temperatures >10,000 K). 
   Electrically conductive metallic hydrogen drives the powerful magnetic dynamo.
3. Dilute, fuzzy rock-ice core of 10-25 Earth masses (revealed by NASA's Juno mission).

Major Moons of Jupiter (Galilean Satellites):
1. Io:
   - Diameter: 3,643 km.
   - Most geologically active body in the Solar System (>400 active volcanoes).
   - Tidal heating driven by Laplace orbital resonance with Europa and Ganymede (4:2:1).
   - Surface dominated by silicate lava flows and sulfur deposits; virtually devoid of impact craters.
2. Europa:
   - Diameter: 3,121.6 km.
   - Surface: smooth ice shell 15-30 km thick crisscrossed by fractures (lineae).
   - Subsurface ocean: global liquid ocean 60-150 km deep, containing 2-3 times the water volume of Earth's oceans.
   - Direct contact between ocean and silicate mantle with possible hydrothermal vents (prime astrobiological candidate).
3. Ganymede:
   - Diameter: 5,268 km (largest moon in the Solar System, larger than Mercury and Titan).
   - Only moon known to generate its own intrinsic dipole magnetic field (from molten iron core).
   - Deep stratified ice layers with saline subsurface ocean under a 100-km ice shell.
4. Callisto:
   - Diameter: 4,820.6 km (third largest satellite in the Solar System).
   - Most heavily cratered surface in the Solar System (giant multi-ring impact basins Valhalla and Asgard).
   - Very low internal activity; partially differentiated structure.


4.2. SATURN

The sixth planet from the Sun, celebrated for its spectacular and complex ring system.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 1,433.5 million km (9.582 AU)
- Orbital period: 29.457 Earth years (10,759 days)
- Eccentricity: 0.0565
- Axial tilt: 26.73° (distinct seasons)
- Rotation period: 10 hours 33 minutes

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 5.6834 × 10^26 kg (95.16 Earth masses)
- Equatorial radius: 60,268 km (9.45 Earth radii)
- Polar radius: 54,364 km (most oblate planet, flattening 0.098)
- Mean density: 0.687 g/cm³ (only planet less dense than liquid water!)
- Surface gravity: 10.44 m/s² (1.065 g)
- Escape velocity: 35.5 km/s
- Atmospheric feature: persistent hexagonal jet stream at the north pole (Saturn's Hexagon), 
  each side measuring ~13,800 km.

Saturn's Ring System:
- Extends from 6,600 km to 120,000 km above Saturn's equator.
- Ring thickness: extraordinarily thin, averaging only 10 to 100 meters!
- Composition: 99% pure water ice with minor traces of silicate dust and tholins.
- Particle sizes: ranging from micrometers to house-sized boulders (mostly 1-10 cm).
- Main ring regions: D, C, B, A, F, G, E (Ring E is fed by the cryogeysers of Enceladus).
- Cassini Division: 4,800 km wide dark gap between the A and B rings cleared by orbital resonance with Mimas.
- Ring mass: ~1.54 × 10^19 kg (~0.4 of Mimas' mass).
- Age of rings (from Cassini data): relatively young, only 10 to 100 million years old.

Moons of Saturn (>145 confirmed moons):
1. Titan:
   - Diameter: 5,149.5 km (second largest moon in the Solar System).
   - Only satellite with a dense atmosphere: surface pressure of 1.45 atm.
   - Atmospheric composition: nitrogen (98.4%), methane (1.4%), ethane, hydrogen.
   - Methane hydrological cycle: methane clouds, rains, drainage networks, and hydrocarbon seas 
     (Kraken Mare, Ligeia Mare).
   - Thick organic haze; subsurface water-ammonia liquid ocean.
2. Enceladus:
   - Diameter: 504.2 km.
   - Highest albedo of any body in the Solar System (reflects ~99% of sunlight, pristine water ice).
   - South polar cryovolcanism: active geysers venting water vapor, ice crystals, salts, and organic molecules 
     from fractures known as "tiger stripes."
   - Global subsurface ocean in direct contact with a porous rocky core (hydrothermal vents, high astrobiological interest).
3. Mimas: diameter 396 km, colossal Herschel Crater (diameter 130 km, gives it the "Death Star" appearance).
4. Iapetus: diameter 1,470 km; extreme brightness dichotomy between dark leading hemisphere (Cassini Regio) 
   and bright trailing hemisphere; prominent equatorial ridge up to 20 km high.


4.3. URANUS

The seventh planet from the Sun, an ice giant characterized by an extreme axial tilt.
Discovered by William Herschel in 1781.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 2,872.5 million km (19.191 AU)
- Orbital period: 84.02 Earth years (30,685 days)
- Eccentricity: 0.0472
- Axial tilt: 97.77° (rotates virtually "on its side" relative to its orbital plane; retrograde rotation).
- Rotation period: 17 hours 14 minutes

Physical Characteristics:
- Mass: 8.6810 × 10^25 kg (14.54 Earth masses)
- Equatorial radius: 25,559 km (4.01 Earth radii)
- Polar radius: 24,973 km
- Mean density: 1.270 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: 8.87 m/s² (0.89 g)
- Escape velocity: 21.3 km/s
- Temperature at 1 bar: ~76 K (-197 °C); lowest recorded tropopause temperature: 49 K (-224 °C), 
  the coldest planetary atmosphere in the Solar System.
- Lowest internal heat emission: emits almost no excess internal heat beyond absorbed solar radiation.

Atmosphere and Magnetic Field:
- Hydrogen (83%), helium (15%), methane (2.3%). Methane absorbs red light, imparting a pale cyan tint.
- Complex magnetic field: tilted at 59° to the rotational axis and offset from the center of the planet by 0.3 planetary radii.

Rings and Moons (28 known moons):
- 13 narrow, dark rings (composed of carbonaceous and organic material).
- Major moons: Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon.
- Miranda: diameter 471 km, wild scrambled geological terrain with fault scarps up to 20 km high (Verona Rupes). 
  Possibly disrupted and reassembled by gravitational accretion.


4.4. NEPTUNE

The eighth and most distant major planet in the Solar System. Discovered mathematically 
through orbital perturbations of Uranus (Johann Galle following calculations by Urbain Le Verrier, 1846).

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 4,495.1 million km (30.07 AU)
- Orbital period: 164.79 Earth years (completed first full orbit since discovery in 2011)
- Eccentricity: 0.00867 (nearly circular)
- Axial tilt: 28.32° (similar to Earth and Mars)
- Rotation period: 16 hours 6 minutes

Physical Characteristics:
- Mass: 1.02413 × 10^26 kg (17.15 Earth masses, denser than Uranus)
- Equatorial radius: 24,764 km (3.88 Earth radii)
- Polar radius: 24,341 km
- Mean density: 1.638 g/cm³ (densest of the four giant planets)
- Surface gravity: 11.15 m/s² (1.14 g)
- Escape velocity: 23.5 km/s
- Internal heat engine: unlike Uranus, Neptune radiates 2.61 times more heat than it receives from the Sun.

Atmosphere and Dynamics:
- Hydrogen (80%), helium (19%), methane (1.5%). Vibrant azure-blue coloration.
- Extreme supersonic winds: fastest planetary winds in the Solar System, reaching speeds of 600 m/s (2,160 km/h), 
  blowing primarily westward (retrograde).
- Massive dynamic storms, such as the Great Dark Spot observed by Voyager 2 in 1989.

Moon Triton:
- Diameter: 2,706.8 km (seventh largest moon in the Solar System).
- Only large moon in the Solar System with a retrograde orbit (moves opposite to planetary rotation), 
  inclined at 157°.
- Origin: captured Kuiper Belt dwarf planet.
- Surface temperature: -235 °C (38 K).
- Active cryovolcanism: nitrogen geysers erupting plumes of nitrogen gas and dark dust up to 8 km high, 
  drifting for hundreds of kilometers downwind.
- Unique "cantaloupe terrain" composed of nitrogen and methane ices.



5. TRANS-NEPTUNIAN REGION: KUIPER BELT AND SCATTERED DISC

The realm beyond Neptune's orbit (from 30 AU to hundreds of AU), preserving remnants 
of the primordial protoplanetary disc rich in volatile ices (water, methane, ammonia, CO, and N2).

Classification of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs):
1. Resonant TNOs (including Plutinos with a 2:3 mean-motion resonance with Neptune).
2. Classical TNOs (Cubewanos, 40-48 AU, non-resonant, low-eccentricity orbits).
3. Scattered Disc Objects (highly eccentric, inclined orbits influenced by Neptune).
4. Detached Objects / Sednoids (perihelia well beyond Neptune's gravitational reach).


5.1. PLUTO AND ITS SYSTEM

Discovered by Clyde Tombaugh in 1930. Considered the 9th planet until the 2006 IAU reclassification as a dwarf planet.
Explored by NASA's New Horizons spacecraft in July 2015.

Orbital Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 5,906.4 million km (39.482 AU)
- Perihelion: 4,437 million km (29.66 AU, inside Neptune's orbit!)
- Aphelion: 7,376 million km (49.30 AU)
- Eccentricity: 0.2488
- Orbital inclination to ecliptic: 17.16°
- Orbital period: 247.94 Earth years
- Stable 2:3 resonance with Neptune prevents collisions.

Physical Parameters:
- Mass: 1.303 × 10^22 kg (0.00218 Earth masses, 0.178 Moon masses)
- Diameter: 2,376.6 km
- Mean density: 1.854 g/cm³ (~70% rock, 30% water ice)
- Surface gravity: 0.62 m/s²
- Surface temperature: 33 to 55 K (-240 to -218 °C)
- Tenuous atmosphere: ~1 Pa (10 microbars), consisting of N2, CH4, and CO.

Geological Discoveries by New Horizons:
- Sputnik Planitia: vast nitrogen-ice glacier spanning ~900,000 km², divided into convective polygonal cells.
- Norgay and Hillary Montes: water-ice mountain ranges rising 3.5-5 km high 
  (at Pluto's temperatures, water ice behaves like rigid granite).
- Cryovolcanic structures (Wright Mons and Piccard Mons).
- Potential subsurface liquid water ocean rich in salts and ammonia.

Moons of Pluto (Binary Planetary System):
1. Charon:
   - Diameter: 1,212 km (over half the diameter of Pluto!).
   - System barycenter lies outside Pluto (approx. 960 km above Pluto's surface).
   - Mutually tidally locked: Pluto and Charon perpetually show the same face to each other.
   - Surface dominated by water ice, featuring the dark reddish polar cap Mordor Macula 
     (photochemically processed tholins captured from Pluto's escaping atmosphere).
   - Colossal tectonic canyon system (Serenity Chasma) up to 9 km deep.
2. Small Moons: Styx, Nix, Kerberos, Hydra (chaotically tumbling non-spherical bodies).


5.2. ERIS (136199 ERIS) AND DYSNOMIA

The most massive dwarf planet in the Solar System. Discovered in 2005 (Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo, David Rabinowitz), 
triggering the IAU 2006 redefinition of a planet.

Parameters:
- Semi-major axis: 67.78 AU (perihelion 37.9 AU, aphelion 97.65 AU)
- Eccentricity: 0.4407
- Inclination: 44.04° (Scattered Disc Object)
- Orbital period: 559 years
- Diameter: 2,326 km (slightly smaller than Pluto, but significantly denser)
- Mass: 1.66 × 10^22 kg (27% more massive than Pluto!)
- Density: 2.52 g/cm³ (high rocky fraction)
- Albedo: 0.96 (one of the most reflective bodies in the Solar System, coated in fresh methane frost)
- Moon: Dysnomia (diameter ~700 km).


5.3. OTHER MAJOR DWARF PLANETS AND TRANS-NEPTUNIAN OBJECTS

Haumea (136108 Haumea):
- Rapid rotation: period of only 3.915 hours.
- Spun into an elongated triaxial ellipsoid: 2,100 × 1,680 × 1,074 km.
- Possesses a ring system (width 70 km at 2,287 km radius) and two moons: Hiʻiaka and Namaka.
- Surface: crystalline water ice.

Makemake (136472 Makemake):
- Classical Kuiper Belt Object (Cubewano), diameter ~1,430 km, covered in methane and ethane frost.
- Satellite: S/2015 (136472) 1 (nicknamed MK2).

Gonggong (225088 Gonggong):
- Scattered disc body, diameter ~1,230 km, dark red surface with water ice and tholins.
- Satellite: Xiangliu.

Quaoar (50000 Quaoar):
- Classical KBO, diameter ~1,110 km.
- Features two inhomogeneous rings orbiting well outside its classical Roche limit.
- Satellite: Weywot.

Sedna (90377 Sedna):
- Detached object with extremely distant orbit: perihelion 76 AU, aphelion 937 AU.
- Orbital period: ~11,400 years.
- Likely originated from the inner Oort Cloud perturbed by passing stars.

Arrokoth (486958 Arrokoth):
- Contact binary visited by New Horizons on January 1, 2019.
- Pristine planetesimal formed by low-velocity accretion in the cold classical Kuiper Belt.



6. FAR REACHES: OORT CLOUD AND COMETS

6.1. THE OORT CLOUD

Hypothesized by Ernst Öpik (1932) and Jan Oort (1950); a vast spherical reservoir of billions of icy planetesimals 
surrounding the Solar System.

Structure:
1. Inner Oort Cloud (Hills Cloud): toroidal region extending from 2,000 to 20,000 AU.
2. Outer Spherical Oort Cloud: extends from 20,000 to 100,000-200,000 AU (up to 1.5-3 light-years, halfway to Alpha Centauri).
- Estimated population: hundreds of billions to trillions of icy bodies (>1 km).
- Estimated mass: several to tens of Earth masses.
- Perturbations: galactic tides and passing stars perturb orbits, sending bodies inward as long-period comets.


6.2. FAMOUS COMETS

Comets are icy bodies ("dirty snowballs" per Fred Whipple) that develop comae and dual tails 
(ion and dust) when heated by solar radiation.

1. Halley's Comet (1P/Halley):
   - Orbital period: 75-76 years (next perihelion: July 28, 2061).
   - First comet identified as periodic (Edmond Halley).
   - Nucleus dimensions: 15 × 8 × 8 km. Albedo only 0.04 (one of the darkest known objects).
2. Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko:
   - Explored by ESA's Rosetta mission and Philae lander (2014-2016).
   - Bilobate shape ("rubber duck") formed by gentle contact binary collision in early Solar System.
   - Discovered glycine (amino acid), phosphorus, molecular oxygen (O2), and complex organics.
3. Comet Hale-Bopp (C/1995 O1):
   - "Great Comet of 1997", visible to the naked eye for a record 18 months.
   - Nucleus diameter: ~60 km. Orbital period: ~2,530 years.


6.3. INTERSTELLAR OBJECTS

Celestial bodies passing through the Solar System on unbound hyperbolic trajectories:
1. 1I/ʻOumuamua (2017):
   - Orbital eccentricity: e = 1.20.
   - Highly elongated shape (length ~400 m, width ~40 m), anomalous non-gravitational acceleration.
2. 2I/Borisov (2019):
   - First confirmed interstellar comet (discovered by Gennadiy Borisov).
   - Eccentricity: e = 3.36. Coma showed high CO abundance, indicating formation in an extremely cold outer protoplanetary disk.



7. BOUNDARIES AND INTERACTION WITH GALAXY: HELIOSPHERE

The Sun continuously emits the solar wind-a stream of protons, electrons, and alpha particles traveling at 300-800 km/s. 
This supersonic plasma forms a vast teardrop-shaped bubble known as the heliosphere.

Key Boundaries of the Heliosphere:
1. Termination Shock:
   - Region where the solar wind drops below the local speed of sound in the interstellar medium (from ~400 km/s to ~100 km/s).
   - Distance: 80-90 AU.
   - Crossed by Voyager 1 in December 2004, Voyager 2 in August 2007.
2. Heliopause:
   - The outer physical boundary of the heliosphere, where the solar wind's dynamic pressure balances the interstellar medium.
   - Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause on August 25, 2012, at 121.6 AU.
   - Voyager 2 crossed it on November 5, 2018, at 119.0 AU.
   - Beyond the heliopause lies pristine interstellar space (dominated by galactic cosmic rays).
3. Gravitational Boundary of the Solar System:
   - Defined by the Sun's Hill Sphere relative to the Milky Way's galactic gravity: 
     approximately 230,000 AU (~1-2 parsecs or 3.6 light-years).
